Who these lessons are for

Most of our guitar and bass students start in one of three places

Guitar is the instrument most students ask for first. Tell us where the student is now and we will match the teacher to it.

Complete beginner

Has never played a note

Beginners start with posture, a handful of chords and finger dexterity, then apply them to a real song quickly. Learning one song you love does more for motivation than a month of exercises.

Young starter

Small hands, short attention span

Ukulele is often the better first instrument for younger children. Four strings, softer nylon, and chords a small hand can actually reach. Many students move to guitar from there.

Teen and adult

Self taught and stuck

If you learned from videos and hit a ceiling, lessons fill the gaps. We fix technique that is holding you back, then get you playing with other people.

Questions parents ask first

Guitar and bass lesson questions, answered

Do I need my own guitar to start?

You will need an instrument to practise on at home, but it does not need to be expensive. Come to the tour first and ask us before you buy anything.

We will tell you what size and type suits the student, which matters more than price. A guitar that is too large is the most common reason a young student gives up early.

Should my child start on guitar or ukulele?

For many children under about eight, ukulele is the easier start. It has four strings instead of six, softer nylon strings, and a smaller neck that a small hand can reach around.

Skills carry across, so students who begin on ukulele often move to guitar later without starting again. We teach both here.

Acoustic or electric?

Either. Some students are motivated by the sound of the music they want to play, and if that is electric then electric is the right place to start. Others prefer acoustic because there is less equipment involved.

The fundamentals we teach in the first year are the same on both.

Do you teach bass guitar?

Yes. Bass lessons run the same way as guitar: private, one-on-one, at the same time each week. Beginners are welcome and you do not need to play guitar first.

Are lessons private or in a group?

Regular weekly lessons are private and one-on-one, by appointment at the same time each week. That way the teacher can work on exactly what that student needs. Makeup classes may be held in a small group.

Can I bring my own songs?

Yes, and we encourage it. Our teachers ask students to bring in their own song ideas. We begin with finger dexterity through scales and chords, then apply those skills to music the student chose.
